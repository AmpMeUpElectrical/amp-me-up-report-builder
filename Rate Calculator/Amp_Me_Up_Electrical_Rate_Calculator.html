Getting this off your laptop

Right now everything lives in this browser only (plus whatever .json files you save). Nothing is uploaded anywhere. Save the file into OneDrive, Google Drive or Dropbox and you can open it on another machine — that's the zero-setup version of "in the cloud". Proper sync, so any device you log into sees the same live numbers, needs a small back end behind it. Say the word and we'll add it.

How the numbers work

No magic in here — this is the sum every accountant does, just kept live.

1. What a person really costs

Base wage (or your own drawings) + annual leave loading + allowances + superannuation + workers' compensation + payroll tax if you're over the threshold + anything else you carry for them (phone, PPE, training, tool allowance). Queensland's payroll tax threshold is $1.3 M of wages, so most small shops leave that at 0.

2. How many hours you can actually sell

Paid hours a year − annual leave − public holidays − sick leave − training = hours actually worked. Then take off travel, quoting, admin, yard and materials runs — what's left is what you can put on an invoice. That's usually 55–70% of the hours you pay for, and it's the number most people get wrong.

3. The rate

Overheads ÷ billable hours + labour cost ÷ billable hours = your breakeven. Below it you're paying for the privilege of working.

Margin and mark-up are not the same thing. A 30% mark-up on $100 gives $130 — and $30 of a $130 sale is only a 23% margin. This tool defaults to margin, because margin is what's left in the business.

4. GST

Every rate here is ex GST. GST isn't yours — you collect it and hand it on. If you're registered, your real cost of an expense is the ex-GST figure, which is why the GST tick-box on the Overheads tab matters. Where an inc-GST figure is useful (what the customer actually sees) it's shown alongside.

What this isn't

It's a costing model, not tax advice, and it doesn't cover materials mark-up, income tax or the cash your business needs to hold. Run any big pricing change past your accountant first.
